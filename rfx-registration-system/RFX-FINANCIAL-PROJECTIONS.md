## 2. The costs and liabilities that eat into revenue

These are the machine's honest drains — every one is already modelled in the
system (referral engine, refund intelligence, prize payouts, staff wages):

| Item | How it works | Typical size |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral commissions** | 15% → 30% of the sale, by tier (1–2 / 3–5 / 6–9 / 10+ active referrals), vested after 30 days ACTIVE | ≈ 15–30% of course revenue |
| **Refunds (cash)** | Rare; fingerprint-scored, fee-heavy, cross-border discouraged (credit is the default route) | ≈ 2–5% of revenue at maturity |
| **Prize money** | Best boy/girl of the year R1,000 each + giveaway pots | ≈ R20k–R60k per year |
| **Merch cost of goods** | T-shirt/hoody/sweatpants at wholesale | ≈ 40–50% of merch price |
| **Staff wages** | Reception rotation (24/7), moderator, finance | set by the founder |
| **Platform / email / hosting** | Firebase, email provider, domains | ≈ R500–R2,000/month |
| **Payment processing** | PayPal / card / EFT fees | ≈ 2–5% of revenue |

**The founder's own rule, encoded:** money that is subject to change (refundable)
isn't the business's yet. A sale only "belongs" after the refund window closes and
the referral commission vests. The projections below respect that — revenue is
counted net of expected refunds and commissions.

## 4. What the founder's cap means for the ceiling

The founder's explicit strategy — **quality over quantity, enrolment capped
(≈2,000 students max)** — is not a revenue ceiling, it's a **yield** strategy:

- Every student at cap is fully supported; churn stays low because support is
  high, so **lifetime value compounds**.
- The referral engine becomes a compounding sales force *inside* the school —
  students market to people they trust, and the business pays only on vested,
  non-refundable results. At 2,000 students with even a modest 30% referral
  rate, ~600 students arrive through trusted peer links per cohort.
- **Net worth at 2,000 is not the end** — the *brand* (the Academy, its
  alumni, its reputation) is an asset that isn't on any ledger. A capped,
  prestigious academy with a 94%-accuracy alumni story is worth multiples of
  its revenue run-rate if the founder ever chooses to sell, franchise or
  expand the model to new markets.
- Diversification runway: at 2,000 students the retained cash (~R6.5M/year)
  funds the founder's ambitions — an office/HQ, a **Reality FX brokerage**
  dream, charity, or simply the machine running forever.

## 5. Honest caveats (so the AGM never gets surprised)

1. **These are planning estimates, not audited accounts.** Real figures depend
   on actual mix, refund rates, staffing costs and the founder's choices.
2. **The system's own end-of-day financial report** (`financialSummary`) is the
   authoritative live number — it reconciles every rand the ledger actually
   holds. Use it for the real P&L; use this document for scenarios.
3. **Refund intelligence keeps drains low by design** — the credit-first policy
   means money mostly stays inside the school (fee-free, no outbound
   transfer), which is exactly why the retained figures above hold up better
   than a naive cash-refund model.
4. **Capacity maths:** the browser demo's ~5MB store caps the demo at a few
   hundred students; **production (Firestore) is effectively unlimited**, so
   the 2,000-student school is a real operating plan, not a demo trick. The
   load test proves the machine holds 2,000 at full strength.
